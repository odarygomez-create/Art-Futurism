"Crafting Beauty: The Enduring Legacy of the Arts and Crafts Movement"

Futurists, like the avant garde, are the progressive movements of the 20th century, as they were brought to bear on the social change. Originally devised by Italian poet Filippo Tommaso Marinetti, whose Futurist Manifesto he put out in 1909, which expressed the essential principles of modern life (speed, progress, and dynamism), the latter movement later developed, along with other experimentalist artists, to create a new visual vocabulary. In this way, Futurism had a great influence in the arts, as can be seen in the visual literature, the literature of stories, music and architecture it tried to give them some rationalism without its preceding epoch's constrictions. In addition, Futurism directly attacked established modalities of artistic practice; even, and in fact, repudiated earlier precedents, insistent in insisting that museums and libraries be destroyed, since such functions are perceived as stasis signals.

The result was that Futurists were swept up by the technological advances of their juncture, with cars and aeroplanes and industrial machinery and so forth. So, they argued, energy and velocity, in addition to these developments, were required to enjoy life in the modern world. So art became animated by movement, and styles evoked motion and chaos but often also incorporated bits and pieces of fractured new images. Futurist painters including Umberto Boccioni, Giacomo Balla, and Carlo Carrà applied these new techniques to depict the dynamism and life in the modern world, mainly in the pictorial medium. A great example is Boccioni's classic sculpture "Unique Forms of Continuity in Space" that portrays continuity through the Futurist concept of fluid lines and a figure in movement. Balla has similarly utilized a same movement as he expressed the power of a dog with its owner on a leash in his piece "Dynamism of a Dog on a Leash."

Here Balla performed quite well in the blurry line and the connected lines between the dog and the owner. He also generally stresses light and speed. That way of being is exactly what he likes. In literature, Marinetti also represents the Futur Movement. As he said it has been ground-breaking in the form of free verse poetry and different kinds of writing for reflecting a rapid tempo, noise, or swift life speed of the age. For example, Marinetti's writing – in which the rhythm and sound was disrupted- all made sense very clearly to listeners.

Futurism was far from limited to Italy: Its impact stretched off across Europe and it influenced other art movements and became an influence. Notable of the Modernists the introduction of Modern technology is witnessed in the work of Russian Constructivists and Dadaists. These aimed to redraw art amid an evolving world. Curiously, the Dada movement had many central ideas to do with futurism. Failing to fit neatly conventional notions of beauty into the ridiculous, chaotic and disillusioned early post-First World War landscape. Yet futurism was affected by its time; its celebrating of speed and technology took a turn for the darker side.

What's more, it was inextricably linked with nationalism; many Futurists backed Italy's joining of World War I in order that the war would prompt social or cultural shifts taking place in Italy. Its focus on combat meant this militaristic fervor was allowed to reassert itself, to its extreme. That has resulted in what is arguably its most negative aspect: Fascism's ascent in Italy after World War I began, and indeed modern art and design are informed by that legacy. As for future movements in modern art, which examined technological influences and modernization. As such, other modern-type movements, like Abstract Expressionism and even elements of Pop Art, grew from the concept of both. Because of this, humanity has examined speed, disruption, and urban life in each of its art forms with a single glance.

Ultimately, Futurism was a movement of revolution which sought to integrate itself with every single transformative advancement that had taken place in modern technology and industry. It presented alternatives to earlier art trends, providing fresh perspectives and opening new frontiers for communication and dialogue, and writing among the visual arts and literature; the aesthetic movement itself created new modes of living. In the end, despite its political and social complexity, the spirit of Futurism is still alive and well in some areas of our lives at this very moment, where work and experience are in constant communication. For a sophisticated look at these fascinating topics, the Museum of Modern Art is home to vast resources on Futurist thought and its enduring social notion.
